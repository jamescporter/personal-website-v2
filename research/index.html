Research

Retinal imaging, machine learning and neurodegenerative brain health.

Can a retinal photograph tell us something meaningful about brain health?

My PhD research investigates whether information in colour photographs of the retina can help estimate retinal structure normally measured by optical coherence tomography (OCT), and how those estimates relate to neurodegenerative disease in population studies.

Why retinal imaging?

The retina is part of the central nervous system and can be examined non-invasively. OCT measures the thickness of individual retinal layers, while colour fundus photography (CFP) captures a two-dimensional image of the back of the eye. The two techniques provide different kinds of information, and fundus photography is more widely available in many settings.

My research asks whether machine learning can extract useful, OCT-related structural information from CFP, and whether such estimates preserve associations between retinal measurements and later neurodegenerative outcomes. An association with future disease does not by itself establish a clinically useful screening test.

From retinal images to interpretable evidence

01

Estimate retinal structure

Use paired CFP and OCT observations from UK Biobank to train models that estimate selected OCT-derived retinal layer thicknesses from fundus photographs. Foundation-model image representations, including RETFound, provide features for downstream estimation.

02

Evaluate anatomical fidelity

Compare estimated measurements with OCT-measured thicknesses using held-out participants and appropriate error, agreement and calibration analyses. Accurate prediction, preservation of population variation and interchangeability with the original measurement are separate questions.

03

Study disease associations

Investigate associations with incident dementia and Parkinson's disease through longitudinal survival analyses. Compare OCT-measured and CFP-estimated measurements, taking account of relevant clinical covariates and the distinction between association and predictive discrimination.

Methods & research practice

Medical imaging

Colour fundus photography, OCT-derived retinal thicknesses, retinal image quality control and participant-level study design.

Machine learning

Pretrained retinal image representations, statistical regression, held-out evaluation and investigation of model calibration.

Longitudinal statistics

Cox proportional-hazards modelling, covariate adjustment, risk discrimination, bootstrap comparisons and sensitivity analyses.

Reproducible computing

Python and R workflows, data provenance, participant-disjoint validation and documented research software.
